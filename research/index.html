Research

My work sits between field recording and the psychogeographical dérive: recording while walking, in a group, and treating listening as a practice that produces shared meaning rather than one that merely documents. Recording the environment collectively turns into a device for reading the acoustic territories of a city — and for making its dominant sonic order visible enough to be questioned.

From there comes the question holding the current work together: whether and how the sonic memory of the field migrates into improvisational practice, and what happens when improvisation becomes a situated, dialogic response to what a space offers and imposes. The structural link between the two moments — the drift and the intervention — is not a proven hypothesis. It is the question the research is built on.

Keywords: psychogeography · field recording · collective listening · urban space · improvisation
