Research

Urban planning is still largely viewed as a top-down discipline, in which transformations of urban space are designed by professionals who rarely consider the active role of human and non-human inhabitants. Recent approaches such as “tactical” and “temporary urbanism,” however, demonstrate how bottom-up interventions can significantly improve urban livability. In this context, artistic practices play a fundamental role in critically challenging the neoliberal approaches of contemporary urban planning. Sound, in particular, profoundly influences the perception and experience of urban environments, and through sound interventions, installations, and sound art, it is possible to transform the atmosphere and social dynamics.

In line with Christopher Dell’s conception of the city as an impromptu process, in which urban actors improvise the space, my research explores the potential of musical improvisation as an ecological and symbiotic practice of interaction with the atmosphere and the urban fabric. Through site-specific performances and participatory workshops, the aim is to investigate how such practices can trigger processes of urban transformation, strengthen social cohesion, and foster new relationships between people and the local area.

Keywords: psychogeography · field recording · collective listening · urban space · improvisation
