The Nomadic Ear: Collective Field Recording and Non-identitarian Listening in Urban Space

Ludovico Franco

Conservatories of Pescara, Ferrara, Trieste and Udine

In SPaMEC 2026: Sound Crossing Communities – Atti della conferenza, edited by Paolo Montella, Materiale Elettrico, Naples 2026, pp. 223–226. ISBN 979-12-986646-0-9.

Download PDF Record on Zenodo

Abstract

This paper explores the practice of field recording as a form of psychogeographical dérive in contemporary urban environments, outlining a conceptual framework for the collective and relational dimensions of listening. It is argued that collective sound dérive constitutes an epistemic and political tool for reading urban acoustic territories and for generating non-identitarian sound communities: forms of belonging founded on the contingent and dialogic experience of the field, rather than on pre-existing identity. When practised collectively, environmental recording serves as a device for the shared production of meaning, capable of rendering the dominant acoustic order visible and challenging it. While existing literature on sonic artistic practices in urban space focuses predominantly on sound installation and soundscape design, this paper serves as a premise for broader research into musical improvisation as a situated and dialogic practice of intervention in urban space.
